Step 1 · Learn

What kind of data is it?

Every statistics question starts with the variables: the things we record about each person or object. Before you can choose a graph or a calculation, you need to know what type of variable you have. VCAA asks this directly almost every year.

By the end of this page you can

  • tell categorical data from numerical data
  • split categorical data into nominal and ordinal
  • split numerical data into discrete and continuous

1. Categorical or numerical?

CategoricalPuts each person or thing into a group or category, like gender (boy, girl) or dominant hand (left, right).
NumericalIs a count or a measurement, like foot length, in centimetres.
Quick test: would it make sense to find the average? You can average foot lengths, so foot length is numerical. You cannot average “left” and “right”, so dominant hand is categorical.

Watch out: numbers used as labels (a postcode, a jersey number, “1 = yes, 2 = no”) are still categorical, because averaging them means nothing.

2. Splitting categorical data

NominalThe categories are just names, with no natural order. Type of tree (fruit, not fruit) is nominal.
OrdinalThe categories have a natural order. Height of tree (short, medium, tall) is ordinal.
The trap VCAA loves: numerical data that has been grouped into ordered categories becomes ordinal. In 2016, 45% of students called age (under 50 years, 50 years or over) nominal. The examiners said:
“The variable age (under 50 years, 50 years or over) is ordinal because the process of allocating each of the people to one of these two categories ‘under 50 years’ or ‘50 years or over’ orders the group of people by age.”
VCAA 2016 Exam 1 report, Question 2

Two categories can still be ordinal: low, high has an order. Two categories like no, yes do not.

3. Splitting numerical data

DiscreteIs counted. It can only take particular values, usually whole numbers. The number of billionaires in a country is discrete.
ContinuousIs measured. It can take any value in a range, limited only by how accurately we measure. Circumference of a tree, in metres is continuous.

Work top to bottom: first decide categorical or numerical, then go one level down.

Step 5 · Reference sheet

Summary

  • Categorical data puts things into groups. Numerical data is a count or a measurement.
  • Ask: does an average make sense? If not, it is categorical, even when the labels are numbers.
  • Nominal categories have no order. Ordinal categories have a natural order, even if there are only two (low, high).
  • Numbers grouped into ordered categories (under 50 years, 50 years or over) are ordinal.
  • Discrete data is counted. Continuous data is measured.

Definitions and tests

TypeMeaningVCAA example
NominalCategories that are just names, no orderexercise type (aerobic, boxing, circuit); snores (no, yes)
OrdinalCategories with a natural orderrecovery time (short, medium, long); blood pressure (low, normal, high)
DiscreteCounted; only particular valuesnumber of billionaires in a country
ContinuousMeasured; any value in a rangefoot length, in centimetres

Decision steps

  1. Does an average make sense? No → categorical. Yes → numerical.
  2. Categorical: is there a natural order? Yes → ordinal. No → nominal.
  3. Numerical: counted → discrete. Measured → continuous.

⚠ Exam traps

  • Number labels (postcodes, 1 = yes) are categorical.
  • Grouped numbers, e.g. age (under 50 years, 50 years or over), are ordinal. Only 31% got this right (VCAA 2016 Exam 1 Q2).
  • Two ordered categories (low, high) are still ordinal. 24% wrongly called no/yes ordinal (VCAA 2020 Exam 1 Q10).
  • Dollar values grouped into ranges (less than $50, more than $50) are categorical. 16% called this numerical (VCAA 2024 Exam 1 Q2).
  • Read “respectively” carefully: the order of the answer matches the order of the variables.
