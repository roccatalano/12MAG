Key formulas
Median positionposition = (n + 1) ÷ 2Median, n evenM = (first middle + second middle) ÷ 2
Interquartile rangeIQR = Q3 − Q1Rangerange = max − min
Lower fenceLF = Q1 − 1.5 × IQR  ·  x < LF → x is an outlierUpper fenceUF = Q3 + 1.5 × IQR  ·  x > UF → x is an outlier

A boxplot is a picture of the five-number summary (min, Q1, M, Q3, max). Each of the four sections holds about 25% of the data. An outlier is drawn as a dot; the whisker stops at the last value inside the fence.

2. Calculate the values of the upper and lower fences, and use these to find any outliers in the ages of the people who attended the party (from above).

IQR = 34 − 20 = 14 · LF = 20 − 1.5 × 14 = −1 · UF = 34 + 1.5 × 14 = 55

Cambridge General Mathematics Units 3&4 · Example 24
Reading shape from a boxplot

Look at where the median sits in the box and which whisker is longer: the long side is the tail. Symmetric: median near the middle, whiskers about equal. Positively skewed: median to the left, long right whisker. Negatively skewed: median to the right, long left whisker. Describe in this order: shape (and outliers) · centre (the median) · spread (IQR and range) · outliers by value.

Cambridge General Mathematics Units 3&4 · Example 26

The boxplot shows the gestation period (completed weeks) for a sample for 1000 babies born in Australia one year. Describe the distribution of gestation period in terms of shape, centre, spread and outliers.

The distribution of gestational period is negatively skewed with several outliers. The distribution is centred at 39 weeks, the median value. The range of the distribution is 17 weeks, but the interquartile range is only 2 weeks. Any gestational period less than 35 weeks or less is considered unusual, with outliers at 25, 26, …, 34 weeks.
