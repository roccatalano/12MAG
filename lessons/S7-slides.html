Key ideas

2C Numerical and categorical variables

Key definitions

Parallel dot plots: a dot plot for each group, one above the other, on the same scale
Back-to-back stem plot: two groups share one stem: leaves to the left for one group, to the right for the other
Parallel boxplots: a boxplot for each group on the same scale (any number of groups)
Association (numerical + categorical): the distribution of the numerical variable (RV) changes from group to group of the categorical variable (EV)

Key formulas

Compare with SOCSShape · Outliers · Centre (median) · Spread (IQR): quote the value for EVERY group
Interquartile range$\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1$
Outlier fencesbelow $Q_1 - 1.5 \times \text{IQR}$ or above $Q_3 + 1.5 \times \text{IQR}$

Comparing a numerical variable across groups

2C Numerical and categorical variables

Does test score (numerical) depend on attended revision class (yes / no, categorical)? Draw one display per group on the same scale, then compare. Click to reveal.

Which display?

Small data sets: parallel dot plots or a back-to-back stem plot
Any size, two or more groups: parallel boxplots

Compare the groups with SOCS

Shape: symmetric / skewed, for each group (hard to see in small data sets)
Outliers: any, and where
Centre: medians: higher or lower?
Spread: IQRs: more or less variable?
Conclude: noticeably different → the variables are associated; much the same → no association
Quote the value for every group (‘M = 26 before, M = 32 after’). Use medians and IQRs, not means: they are not affected by outliers.

Example 9

Using a parallel dot plot to identify and describe associations

Cambridge · Example 9
The parallel dot plots below display the distribution of the number of sit-ups performed by 15 people before and after they had completed a gym program.
Do the parallel dot plots support the contention that the number of sit-ups performed is associated with completing the gym program? Write a brief explanation that compares the distributions.
Key idea
Compare with SOCS: medians and IQRs for every group
differences → associated
RV: sit-ups · EV: gym program. 15 values: M = 8th dot, Q1 = 4th, Q3 = 12th. Before: M = 26, IQR = 30 − 24 = 6; after: M = 32, IQR = 36 − 26 = 10.
Centre: The median number of sit-ups performed after attending the gym program (M = 32) is higher than the median number of sit-ups performed before attending the gym program (M = 26).
Spread: The variability in the number of sit-ups has also increased from IQR = 6 to IQR = 10.
✓ Answer Thus we can conclude that the number of sit-ups performed is associated with completing the gym program.
Cambridge General Mathematics Units 3&4, 2C Example 9 (pp. 123–124; dot plots redrawn; report in SOCS order)

Example 11

Comparing distributions across two groups using parallel boxplots

Cambridge · Example 11
Use the following parallel boxplots to compare the pulse rates (in beats/minute) for a group of 70 male students and 90 female students.
Key idea
Compare with SOCS: medians and IQRs for every group
differences → associated
Shape: The distributions of resting pulse rates for both male and female students were approximately symmetric.
Outliers: One male was found to have an extremely low pulse rate of 40, while another had an extremely high pulse rate of 120.
Centre: On average, the resting pulse rate for males is lower (median: male = 65, female = 72)
Spread: and less variable than that for females (IQR: male = 10, female = 15).
✓ Answer There is an association between resting pulse rate and gender.

Example 12

Comparing distributions across more than two groups using parallel boxplots

Cambridge · Example 12
Use the parallel boxplots below to compare the salary distribution for workers in a certain industry across four different age groups: 20–29 years, 30–39 years, 40–49 years and 50–65 years.
Key idea
Compare with SOCS: medians and IQRs for every group
differences → associated
Shape: The shape of the distribution of salaries changes with age group, from symmetric for 20−29-year-olds, to progressively more positively skewed as age increases.
Outliers: There are no outliers in the 20-29 and 30-39 age group. Outliers also begin to appear at $110 000 for the 40-49 age group, and at $119 000, $126 000 and $140 000 for the 50-65 age group.
Centre: The median salaries increase across the age groups, from $64 000 for 20−29 year-olds to $72 000 for 50−65 year-olds.
Spread: The salaries also became more variable, with the IQR increasing from around $12 000 for 20−29-year-olds to around $20 000 for 50−65-year-olds.
✓ Answer In this industry there is an association between salary and age group.

Writing a report from parallel boxplots

Edrolo 2B WE4
The following parallel boxplots display the distribution of prices for a 3-bedroom house in three different locations in NSW: ‘rural’, ‘outer suburbs’ and ‘inner suburbs’.
Identify the association between price and location.
Write a report referencing shape, centre and spread.
Key idea
Compare with SOCS: medians and IQRs for every group
differences → associated
Shape: In relation to shape, the distribution of price is positively skewed in locations further from a city in NSW and approximately symmetrical in the inner suburbs.
Centre: In relation to centre, the median price increases as the location is closer to a city in NSW.
Spread: In relation to spread, the range and IQR of price tends to be larger in locations closer to a city in NSW.
✓ Answer The parallel boxplots show that price and location are associated. The closer a 3-bedroom house is to a city in NSW, the higher the price of the house.
