The normal distribution and the 68–95–99.7% rule

A symmetric, bell-shaped model: mean = median, at the centre; the standard deviation sets the width. Approximately 68%, 95% and 99.7% of values lie within 1, 2 and 3 standard deviations of the mean. Each tail: beyond 1 SD 16% · beyond 2 SD 2.5% · beyond 3 SD 0.15%. Use symmetry: 50% either side of the mean.

Key formulas

withininterval%
1 SDx̄ − s to x̄ + s68%
2 SDx̄ − 2s to x̄ + 2s95%
3 SDx̄ − 3s to x̄ + 3s99.7%
Standardised score (z-score): how many SDs a value is above (+) or below (−) the mean.
\[z=\dfrac{x-\bar x}{s} \qquad x=\bar x+z\times s\] Tail → z: 16% above → z = 1 · 2.5% above → z = 2 · 0.15% above → z = 3 (negative for tails below).

Populations and samples

  • Population: every member of the group we want to know about.
  • Sample: a subset of the population that is actually measured.
  • Random sample: every member has the same chance of being picked.
Measuring a whole population is usually too slow or too costly, so we measure a sample and use its mean \(\bar x\) and standard deviation s to estimate the population's.

Picture it

blue: the population · orange: a random sample of 10

Why random?

A random sample is fair: no part of the population is favoured, so the sample statistics give fair estimates. A sample chosen for convenience (e.g. only your friends, only one class) can be biased.

Spot it in VCAA questions

"A sample of 250 … drawn at random from a population …": the numbers you are given (mean, SD, five-number summary) describe the sample. GM uses the sample standard deviation (sx) on CAS.
