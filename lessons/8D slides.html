Key definitions

Total paidall the payments added together
Total interestwhat the loan cost (or the annuity earned) on top of the principal

The final payment

The last payment clears the balance exactly

  • the regular payment rarely divides the debt exactly, so the last payment is adjusted
  • Final payment = balance still owing + one period of interest on it
  • its principal reduction = the whole balance, so the new balance is 0
Final paymentbalance + interest on it
Balance before the last payment: $1000 at 1% per period
Interest = 1% × 1000 = $10
Final payment = 1000 + 10 = $1010
Don’t just pay the balance: it earns one more period of interest first.

    Total payments and total interest

    Add up the payments; the extra over the principal is interest

    • Total payments = regular payment × how many + the final payment
    • Total interest = total payments − principal (loan or annuity)
    • check: add up the interest column
    Total interesttotal payments − principal
    3 payments of $400, then a final $410, on a $1500 loan
    Total paid = 3 × 400 + 410 = $1610
    Interest = 1610 − 1500 = $110
    Use the adjusted final payment, not another regular one.

      Plotting from an amortisation table

      One point per payment

      • payment number across, the amount ($) up
      • loan: interest falls each payment, principal reduction rises (the balance is shrinking)
      • investment with additions: the interest rises each payment
      Loaninterest ↓, principal reduction ↑
      Two series on one graph: use two colours and a key.
