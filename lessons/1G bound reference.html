1GThe five-number summary and the boxplotStudy Design: the five-number summary and boxplots (including the use of the lower fence (Q1 – 1.5 × IQR) and upper fence (Q3 + 1.5 × IQR) to identify and display possible outliers)

Five-number summary and boxplot

Five-number summary: minimum, Q1, median, Q3, maximum.
Boxplot: a picture of it. The box runs Q1 to Q3 (the middle 50%) with a line at the median; the whiskers run to the smallest and largest values that are not outliers. Each of the four sections holds about 25% of the data, however long it looks: a long whisker means spread out, not more values.

Key formulas

\[\text{IQR}=Q_3-Q_1 \qquad \text{range}=\max-\min\] \[\text{LF}=Q_1-1.5\times\text{IQR} \qquad \text{UF}=Q_3+1.5\times\text{IQR}\] Outlier: x < LF → outlier · x > UF → outlier. Drawn as a dot; the whisker stops at the last value inside the fence.

CAS 4 · 19 marks

Q1 = 23, Q3 = 35, IQR = 12
LF = 23 − 1.5 × 12 = 5 · UF = 35 + 1.5 × 12 = 53
3 < 5 = LF → 3 is an outlier; lower whisker stops at 21.

  2. SetGraph: Draw On · Type MedBox · XList main\marks · Freq 1.
  3. Tick Show Outliers; Set; plot.
  4. Tap Trace; move with the cursor arrows.

Describe in this order

Shape (and outliers) · centre (the median) · spread (IQR and range) · outliers by value.
