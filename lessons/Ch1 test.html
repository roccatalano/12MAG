Question 173 marks

The masses of packets of rice crackers filled by a machine at a factory in Dandenong are approximately normally distributed with a mean of 250 g and a standard deviation of 4 g. Use the 68 − 95 − 99.7% rule to answer the following.

aDetermine the percentage of packets with a mass between 242 g and 254 g. (1 mark)
242 = μ − 2σ, 254 = μ + σ: 47.5% + 34% = 81.5%
bIn a batch of 2000 packets, how many packets would be expected to have a mass less than 246 g? (1 mark)
246 = μ − σ: 16% × 2000 = 320
cPackets with a mass less than 238 g are rejected. How many of the 2000 packets would be expected to be rejected? (1 mark)
238 = μ − 3σ: 0.15% × 2000 = 3

Question 186 marks

The times taken by commuters to cycle to work in Melbourne are approximately normally distributed. Of these commuters, 16% take longer than 38 minutes and 2.5% take less than 20 minutes.

aDetermine the mean and the standard deviation of the cycling times. (3 marks)
μ + σ = 38 and μ − 2σ = 20, so 3σ = 18
Mean = 32 minutes, standard deviation = 6 minutes
bCalculate the standardised score of a commuter who takes 41 minutes to cycle to work. (1 mark)
(41 − 32) ÷ 6 = 1.5
cSam says that a cycling time of 50 minutes would be very unusual for these commuters. Use the 68 − 95 − 99.7% rule to decide whether Sam is correct. (2 marks)
Sam is correct. A time of 50 minutes is three standard deviations above the mean, so only about 0.15% of commuters take longer than this.

Question 196 marks

The number of hours of part-time work completed in one week by 15 Year 12 students at a school in Geelong is shown below.

Hours worked467889101011121213141528

aDetermine the median and the interquartile range (IQR) of the number of hours worked. (2 marks)
Median = 10 hours, IQR = 13 − 8 = 5 hours
bShow that the student who worked 28 hours is an outlier. (1 mark)
The upper fence is 13 + 1.5 × 5 = 20.5 hours and 28 > 20.5, so 28 hours is an outlier.
cDescribe the shape of the distribution. (1 mark)
Positively skewed
dThe mean number of hours worked is 11.13, rounded to two decimal places. Is the mean or the median the better measure of the centre of this data? Explain your answer. (2 marks)
The median. The distribution is positively skewed with an outlier, which increases the mean, whereas the median is not affected by the outlier.
