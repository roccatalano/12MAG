Key ideas

Key definitions and formulas

Key definitions

Stagea stretch of time where the rate and payment stay the same
Changea new interest rate or a new regular payment part-way through
Lump suma one-off extra payment that reduces a loan balance
Unroundedkeep every in-between value in full until the final answer

Key formulas

Two stagesFV of stage 1 → PV of stage 2keep the sign: an investment stays negative, a loan stays positive
N per stageN = years × PpYstage 2 only counts the payments left
Final paymentloan: regular + FVFV positive (overpaid) → final is smaller
Lump sumbalance now − PV neededPV needed = what the rest of the payments can repay

Unit cost depreciation (Year 11)

Anton is a musician, and he purchases a minivan for $64 000 to transport equipment.
After four years, the value of the minivan is $40 000.
The minivan travelled an average of 8000 km in each of the four years since it was purchased.
Assume that the value of the minivan has been depreciated using the unit cost method of depreciation.
By how much is the value of the minivan reduced per kilometre travelled? (1 mark)
  1. 1Total drop64 000 − 40 000 = 24 000
  2. 2Total km4 × 8000 = 32 000 km
  3. 3Per km24 000 ÷ 32 000 = 0.75
  1. ✓AnswerThe value drops by $0.75 per kilometre.

When the conditions change

Split it into stages

  • stage 1: the time before the change: solve for FV
  • stage 2: FV becomes the new PV; change N, I% or PMT
  • do not round in between: banks only round at the end
Two stagesFV of stage 1 → PV of stage 2
ChangeSame paymentTo keep the same end date
Loan rate risestakes longer, more interestpay more each month
Loan rate fallsrepaid sooner, less interestpay less each month
Annuity rate fallsruns out soonertake less, or add a lump sum
Investment payment risesgrows fasterreach the target sooner
Use the unrounded FV (copy it, don’t retype it).

Finding the value of an investment when the regular payment changes

Derek invests $50 000 into a compound interest investment paying 6.1% per annum, compounding annually. Derek invests an additional $8000 per year immediately after interest is calculated.
After five years, Derek increases his additional investment to $10 000 per year.
Calculate the value of Derek’s investment after twelve years (in total).
  1. 1Stage 1N = 5 · I = 6.1 · PV = −50 000 · PMT = −8000 · FV = ? · PpY = CpY = 1
  2. 2SolveFV = 112 414.364… (5 years before the change)
  3. 3Stage 2N = 7 · I = 6.1 · PV = −112 414.364… · PMT = −10 000 · FV = ? · PpY = CpY = 1
  4. 4SolveFV = 254 343.797… (7 years after the change)
  1. ✓AnswerThe value of Derek’s investment is $254 343.80.

Finding the final payment when the interest rate changes

Cilian takes out a reducing balance loan of $35 000 with a bank that charges an interest rate of 5.9% per annum, compounding monthly. He makes a payment of $1000 each month to pay off the loan.
  1. aHow much does Cilian still owe after 21 months?
  2. bThe bank changes the interest rate for Cilian’s loan after 21 months to 6.4% per annum compounding monthly. Cilian’s monthly payments remain unchanged. To ensure the loan is fully repaid, the final repayment will be lower. If Cilian manages to pay off the rest of the loan, how much interest will he pay in total? Use the rounded value found in part a.
  1. 1aN = 21 · I = 5.9 · PV = 35 000 · PMT = −1000 · FV = ? · PpY = CpY = 12 → FV = −16 731.708…
  2. 2b · NN = ? · I = 6.4 · PV = 16 731.71 · PMT = −1000 · FV = 0 · PpY = CpY = 12 → N = 17.57…: 17 full payments + 1 final
  3. 3b · LeftN = 17 · I = 6.4 · PV = 16 731.71 · PMT = −1000 · FV = ? · PpY = CpY = 12 → −570.161…
  4. 4b · FinalN = 1, PV = 570.161…, FV = 0 → PMT = −573.20
  5. 5b · Interest21 × 1000 + 17 × 1000 + 573.20 − 35 000
  1. ✓Answers
    aCilian still owes $16 731.71.
    bTotal paid $38 573.20, so interest = $3573.20.

Ken has borrowed $70 000 to buy a new caravan.
He will be charged interest at a rate of 6.9% per annum, compounding monthly.
Ken will make monthly repayments of $800.

After three years, Ken will make a lump sum payment of $L in order to reduce the balance of his loan.
This lump sum payment will ensure that Ken’s loan is fully repaid in a further three years.
Ken’s repayment amount and interest remains the same.
What is the value of Ken’s lump sum payment, $L? Round to the nearest dollar. (2 marks)
