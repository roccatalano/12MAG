Today’s lesson

Today’s lesson

Learning intentions

  • To be able to establish when multiplication of two matrices can be undertaken.
  • To be able to undertake the multiplication of matrices.
  • To be able to understand that the order of multiplication of matrices is important.
  • To be able to apply multiplication of matrices to practical problems.
  • To be able to use your CAS calculator to undertake multiplication of matrices.
  • To be able to understand the role of 'summing matrices'.
  • To be able to work with calculations involving powers of matrices.

Success criteria

  • I can determine if the product of two given matrices is defined.
  • I can determine the order of a matrix product.
  • I can multiply a row matrix by a column matrix by hand.
  • I can multiply a rectangular matrix by a column matrix by hand.
  • I can use summing matrices to sum the rows or columns of a matrix.
  • I can undertake multiplications of matrices to solve practical problems.
  • I can evaluate matrix expressions involving powers.

When is a product defined? What is its order?

Write the orders side by side

  • Rule 1: AB is defined only if columns of A = rows of B (the inside numbers)
  • Rule 2: AB has the rows of A and the columns of B (the outside numbers)
  • order matters: AB can be defined when BA is not
Defined?(m × n)(n × s)
OrderAB is m × s
(2 × 3)(3 × 1) → inside equal → defined
(2 × 3)(3 × 1) → order 2 × 1
(3 × 1)(2 × 2) → 1 ≠ 2 → not defined
Write the orders under each matrix BEFORE you multiply anything.

Summing matrices

A matrix of 1s adds things up

  • sum the rows: post-multiply (on the right) by a COLUMN of 1s (n × 1)

A2 = A × A

  • A2 = A × A, A3 = A × A × A, …

  • A2 is NOT squaring each element
  • keep the order of operations: (AB)2 = (AB)(AB)
