Today’s lesson

Today’s lesson

Learning intentions

  • To be able to introduce the scatterplot for displaying data from two numerical variables.
  • To be able to construct a scatterplot using a CAS calculator.
  • To be able to use a scatterplot to identify an association between two variables.
  • From the the scatterplot, be able to classify an association according to: direction, which may be positive or negative; form, which may be linear or non-linear; strength, which may be weak, moderate or strong.

Success criteria

  • I can construct a scatterplot using a CAS calculator.
  • I can classify the direction, form and strength of an association from a scatterplot.

Key ideas

Key definitions and formulas

Key definitions

Scatterplota plot of paired numerical data: EV on the horizontal (x) axis, RV on the vertical (y) axis; one point per case
Directionpositive (RV tends to increase as EV increases), negative (RV tends to decrease) or none
Formlinear (points follow a straight line) or non-linear (a curve)
Strengthstrong, moderate or weak: how tightly the points cluster about a trend line
Outliera point that does not fit the pattern of the rest

Key formulas

Describe an associationDirection · Form · Strength+ any outliers, then a sentence in context
Sentence templateThere is a strong, positive, linear association between RV and EVe.g. ‘taller players tend to be heavier’

The graph below shows parallel boxplots for the average heights of adult males, in centimetres, in 208 countries in 1896, 1946 and 1996.
Which one of the following statements can be concluded from the boxplots?
A.The range of the average heights for males in the 208 countries was greater in 1946 than in 1996.
B.The average adult male height was greater than 170 cm in 1996 for more than 100 of the countries observed.
C.The largest average adult male height observed in 1896 was less than the median of the average male heights observed in 1996.
D.No countries out of any of the 208 countries observed had an average adult male height less than 155 cm in 1896.
  1. 1Test B1996 median ≈ 171 cm > 170 cm: half of 208 = 104 countries are above it ✓
  2. 2Check A, C, DA: range 1946 ≈ 18 < 1996 ≈ 23 ✗ · C: 1896 max ≈ 172 > 1996 median ≈ 171 ✗ · D: 1896 min ≈ 153 < 155 ✗
  1. ✓Answer · BMore than 100 countries had an average above 170 cm in 1996: B.

      Direction of an association

      First: is there a clear pattern?

      No pattern (a random scatter) → no association. A clear pattern → describe its direction:

      • positive: points trend upwards left to right (RV tends to increase as EV increases)
      • negative: points trend downwards left to right
      Directionpositive · negative · none
      positive
      negative
      Always say what the direction means in context: ‘taller players tend to be heavier’.

        Form of an association

        Linear or non-linear?

        • linear: the points tend to follow a straight line
        • non-linear: the points tend to follow a curved line

        For now we analyse linear associations; non-linear data can often be made linear (Chapter 4).

        Formlinear · non-linear
        linear
        non-linear
        Imagine a line through the points: if a curve fits better, the form is non-linear.

            Strength of an association

            How much scatter?

            • strong: tightly clustered about a trend line
            • moderate: broadly clustered about a trend line
            • weak: loosely clustered about a trend line
            • no pattern: no association
            Strengthstrong · moderate · weak
            strong positive
            strong negative
            moderate positive
            moderate negative
            weak positive
            weak negative
            Describe in the order direction, form, strength, then outliers: ‘a strong, negative, linear association’.
