1FMeasures of centre and spreadStudy Design: summary of the distributions of numerical variables; the sample mean and standard deviation and their use in comparing data distributions in terms of centre and spread

Two questions about any distribution

CentreSpread
skewed or outliersmedian M: the middle valueIQR: spread of the middle 50%
symmetric, no outliersmean \(\bar x\): the balance pointstandard deviation s: spread around the mean
They come in pairs: median with IQR, mean with standard deviation. Range = largest − smallest (uses only two values).

Mean or median?

The mean and the median are both measures of the centre of a distribution. If the distribution is symmetric and there are no outliers, either the mean or the median can be used to indicate the centre of the distribution. If it is clearly skewed and/or there are outliers, it is more appropriate to use the median. The median is resistant: changing the largest value moves the mean but not the median.

Key formulas

Median at position \(\tfrac{n+1}{2}\) in the ordered data (n odd: middle value; n even: average of the two middle values)
Q1 = median of the lower half · Q3 = median of the upper half (n odd: leave the median out of both halves)
\[\text{IQR}=Q_3-Q_1 \qquad R=\text{largest}-\text{smallest}\] \[\bar x=\dfrac{\sum x}{n} \qquad s=\sqrt{\dfrac{\sum (x-\bar x)^2}{n-1}}\] s is roughly the typical distance of the values from the mean.
